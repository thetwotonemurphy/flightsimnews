SmartLink · 1:31:35

SmartLink wants to become the flight-sim control room

Developer Kanzooie joined Murph for the night’s centrepiece: a long-form first look at SmartLink, a free utility currently in beta that tries to pull a surprising amount of the flight-sim workflow into one place.

The demonstration moved from Dispatch and SimBrief planning into a hangar and scenery library, map filters for installed airports and World Update POIs, WingLink group tools, VoiceLink commands, configurable overlays, landing cards, debriefs, app-launch routines and a Sim Health dashboard. SmartLink can also activate and deactivate Community-folder content through its own library workflow and build reusable sets for different kinds of flying.

The clever part is how the pieces connect. A voice phrase can trigger a keyboard shortcut; routines can react to events such as wheels-up, altitude or engine start; a routine can then change a camera, control sim functions, launch software or display information. Kanzooie also showed exportable routines and community-shared aircraft control mappings.

Murph’s Take

“This replaces AVX for me straight away. This replaces any sort of add-ons linker… I genuinely use this before every single flight.”

“It requires zero training to just start… and then you kind of figure out and learn different things as you go.”

Murph’s own testing found the utility extremely light on system resources, while Kanzooie stressed that keeping it lightweight is a core design goal. Sim Health is explicitly opt-in and is intended to make crashes and stutters easier to interpret, including whether other participating users have seen the same crash pattern. SmartLink remains beta software, with feedback and bug reporting built directly into the app.

Hands on

Three add-ons under the microscope
EV-97 Ultralight · 2:32:07

Small, simple and pleasant to fly — but clearly rooted in 2020

Just Flight supplied a pre-release build of Italian Studio Works’ EV-97, a two-seat Czech ultralight built around a Rotax engine. The product page describes a model developed for MSFS 2020 and compatible with 2024, with 4K PBR textures, seven liveries and flight dynamics tested by real EV-97 pilots.

Murph liked the basic handling and the character of the little Eurostar, but the preview also exposed its age: some visual presentation and cockpit elements felt much more like a 2020-era product than a native 2024 aircraft. His verdict was therefore heavily dependent on price and final polish.

Murph’s Take

“The flight dynamics feel pretty good… it needs a bit more polish, I think, for 2024.”

MM Simulations Skiathos · 2:47:24

Skiathos looks the part — until the LODs start disappearing

The famous Greek island airport has all the ingredients: a dramatic beachfront approach, detailed surroundings, animated life and attractive night lighting. At close range Murph found plenty to like.

The problem was aggressive level-of-detail behaviour. Cars and even sizeable buildings were visibly popping into existence at distances that would be noticeable from an aircraft on approach. Murph reproduced the issue repeatedly and said he would report it back to MM Simulations.

Murph’s Take

“Night lighting is nice. LOD is absolutely shagged… this needs, in my opinion, a dramatic fix.”

Fly 2 High William P. Hobby · 2:57:14

Houston Hobby steals the hands-on show

Fly 2 High’s William P. Hobby immediately landed better. Murph toured the FBOs, terminal interiors, animated passengers, screens, GA areas and Southwest facilities before switching to night and rain.

The visual density, reflections and night lighting impressed throughout, and the airport also carried a bit of personal nostalgia from FlightSimExpo in Houston. Two imperfections did surface: the terminal lacks ambient sound, and rain was visibly falling inside parts of the interior. Neither stopped this from being the strongest scenery of the night.

Murph’s Take

“This is probably the best add-on we had a look at… I’m delighted I added this to me library.”
