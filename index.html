Headline

The superjumbo arrives
INIBUILDS · 31:03

The A380 is finally here

After years of development, iniBuilds’ A380 arrived in the MSFS 2024 Marketplace. Murph’s first impression was emphatic: the launch response had been strong, the aircraft had serious systems depth, both engine options, a richly modelled flight deck and enough automation to make the enormous Airbus approachable without stripping away the challenge.

Performance was the obvious watch-point for an aircraft of this scale, and Murph highlighted the options to reduce the load, including cabin and visual-effect controls. His own flights — including the entirely sensible decision to take the first one into Madeira — had already convinced him this was iniBuilds’ biggest aircraft achievement yet.

Murph's Take

“This has been a cracking release… it’s just a great aircraft. It’s enjoyable to fly. It’s a challenge. There’s depth there if you want it.”

BLUEBIRD 757 · 35:21

October 23 is finally on the calendar

BlueBird put a date on the 757: 23 October. Murph unpacked the unusual part of the delay — the aircraft itself is described as being in final-touch territory, while the studio has also been building the commercial infrastructure around its first major release and working through changes introduced by Sim Update 7.

The $80 package is planned with -200 and -300 passenger variants, Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney engine options, and coverage for both 2020 and 2024; the fully native 2024 version is planned as a later free upgrade.

Murph's Take

The hype has been enormous, and that cuts both ways. Murph is excited — the 757 was the first commercial aircraft he flew on — but after years of “coming soon”, expectations are now sky-high.

EMERGENCY DISPATCHER PRO · 1:05:05

From solo rescue calls to an operations room

EmergencyDispatcherPro’s Global Missions and multiplayer update changes the scale of the add-on. Co-op crews can now answer the same incident, while the browser-based Command Center lets a dispatcher create and assign incidents, track aircraft and coordinate multiple active operations. Remote Medics can even join the patient-care side without owning EDPro.

“This is genius.”

That was Murph’s reaction to the command-centre concept — and the role-play possibilities were obvious: who is closest, who has the right equipment, who actually has the fuel to take the call?

SayIntentions.AI

Voices, charity and the cabin
FLIGHT DECK → PASSENGERS · 1:34:12

“Just stall them.”

SayIntentions.AI’s new passenger-announcement capability was the perfect invitation for Murph to test the limits. From the A380 flight deck he asked his co-pilot to brief passengers using the SimBrief plan — departure, destination, cruise altitude and flight time — and then, naturally, asked him to stall a delay with a joke.

The co-pilot obliged. The resulting “grounded” dad joke was followed, in Murph’s imagination, by roughly 390 passengers slapping their foreheads.

“This is genius, right? So hats off to SayIntentions.AI. Brilliant.”

The more useful side is dynamic: delays, turbulence, diversions and other events can now be passed from the flight deck to the cabin, with context already available from the filed flight.

Watch the live demo
ROUTE 66 SKY RELAY · 1:28:48

Route 66 takes to the sky for Orbis

This is more than another group flight. On 26–27 September, the Route 66 Sky Relay turns the classic Chicago-to-Los Angeles route into a 24-hour community relay supporting Orbis and its Flying Eye Hospital. Eleven creators will carry the route across the United States, with Murph scheduled to launch the event on the opening Chicago-to-St. Louis leg.

The important bit: the community is invited. You can fly a single leg, jump in for several, or attempt the whole relay — and participation is not locked behind SayIntentions.AI. The aim is to bring the wider flight-sim community together around the route while putting Orbis and its sight-saving work front and centre.

Murph's Take

A proper community event: multiple streamers, an iconic route, everybody welcome, and a reason for doing it beyond simply logging another flight.

The toolbox

How Murph actually uses it
PARALLEL 42 · 1:44:51

ChasePlane: consistency before cinematics

The deep dive began with cameras. Murph’s real reason for ChasePlane is not just pretty wing shots: it is standardisation. The same controls and broadly the same camera positions follow him from aircraft to aircraft — and even between MSFS and X-Plane — so learning a new cockpit means less relearning of the interface.

From there he demonstrated view groups, physics, keybinds, Stream Deck control, community camera imports, cinematics, head tracking, the browser remote and the newer spotting tools — including SayIntentions.AI traffic.

Murph's Take

“It is a remarkable bit of kit.” The best camera system is the one that disappears into muscle memory.

FLOW PRO · 2:16:53

Flow Pro: the wheel Murph misses when it isn't there

Flow is the other half of Murph’s everyday setup: fast access to time, weather, cameras, radios, checklists, doors, Twitch interactions and third-party widgets without constantly fighting the default toolbar. The TTM wheel, ghost wheels and community widgets showed how personal the setup can become.

There are limitations inherited from MSFS 2024 — multiplayer toggles and some data points simply are not exposed to developers yet — and Murph also discussed reports of FPS loss, with Portal/friend-list activity one area he has been investigating.

“Flow Pro is one of these things, I think, you miss it if you don’t have it.”

One more experiment

OptiScaler / neural rendering
DLSS / OPTISCALER · 2:49:26

“I break the things so you don’t have to.”

Murph closed the practical part of the show by reporting back on his OptiScaler experiment. Compared with his earlier DLSS 5 tests, he was seeing a much smoother result and lower GPU load on his own RTX 5090 setup. He credited Roy for the pointer and Kenko for a straightforward installation video.

Crucially, this was presented as a personal visual preference rather than a universal recommendation. Murph said he would keep the on-screen DLSS marker visible whenever he uses it so viewers know the image is being altered.

“I think it looks absolutely shagging deadly.”
