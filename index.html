The main event

Hands-on review · Not a product endorsement
PropAir Flight · 1:54:12

The Cheyenne III has potential. But Murph found serious problems.

What began as a proper flight test became the strongest opinion of the night. Murph explored the Cheyenne III’s cockpit, engine and aircraft behaviour, and its SkyPass training layer. The experience raised questions about the aircraft’s systems, flight modelling, value and release readiness — even after a patch.

During testing, Murph demonstrated an artificial-horizon problem and questioned the aircraft’s ability to remain inverted without an apparent fuel-flow consequence. These were observations from this livestream, not a claim that every user or version will encounter the same behaviour.

Murph’s Take

“The aircraft shouldn't have been released in this state.”

His conclusion was critical but not dismissive of the developer: he wants PropAir Flight to fix the problems so he can revisit the Cheyenne in a few months and tell a better story. The marketing emphasis on SkyPass did not, in his view, make up for the aircraft’s shortcomings.

Tailstrike and TFDi launch shared persistent MD-11 states

The free Tailstrike integration is live for PC users of MSFS 2020 and 2024. TFDi MD-11 pilots can inherit the previous pilot’s cockpit configuration; owners of Extended Simulation can also carry forward wear and failures. Pilots can choose full, basic or no state synchronisation.

56:17

TerraBuilder SpacePort: a Space Shuttle launch inside MSFS

TerraBuilder demonstrated its in-development SpacePort module for Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020 and 2024, with launch preparation at Kennedy Space Center Pad 39A, a Space Shuttle ascent, solid rocket booster separation and a flight toward low Earth orbit. The developer reports working powered-ascent guidance and a mission sequence extending to payload deployment. This is work-in-progress footage, not a released, fully playable Shuttle simulation. Murph watched the longer launch sequence on stream, called the result “wild” and “class looking,” and was enthusiastic about its future possibilities, while questioning how much direct flying the current demonstrations actually show.
